Sensor Fusion Tutorial: Machine Learning at the Edge. LAFusion Bootcamp, By Jeremy Ellis

Welcome to the LAFusion 2026 Bootcamp! As AI and automation rapidly reshape every modern career path, foundational digital literacy requires looking beyond cloud black boxes to understand how physical hardware and client-side intelligence actually connect. This page is the whole tutorial: slides, videos and every link you need.
The tools you will see come from webmcu-ai, open-source projects that show the whole machine-learning pipeline, from sensor to decision. The repositories are at https://github.com/webmcu-ai
This page is at https://hpssjellis.github.io/lafusion-ellis-tutorial/index.html

At a glance: sensors in, decisions out, the whole pipeline visible

  • The big idea: look inside the box. Single-file firmware and single-file webpages let you read every stage of the pipeline: sensors, features, model, decision, action. You can inspect it, change it and explain it.
  • My path: a decade of TinyML and robotics teaching (Maker100 Leaders Robotics), then WebAI solutions that run as plain web pages (beginner TensorFlow.js examples), then both combined in the GitHub organization webmcu-ai.
  • What you need: a laptop with Chrome or Edge and a curious mind. A board is a bonus, not a requirement: the five videos in the playlist show every step.
  • The videos: watch them in order. Video 5, sensor fusion with the Nano 33 BLE Sense and a webpage for ML training, is probably the most important one.
  • Context: this tutorial connects hands-on classroom projects with LAFusion's interest in information fusion and sensor fusion. The on-device ideas are described in two open arXiv preprints (2604.23012, 2604.22834).
  • Contact: questions, ideas and collaborations are very welcome: Jeremy Ellis on LinkedIn

My tools are not better than professional tools, and they might have some issues. What they offer is a pipeline you can see from start to finish, and a starting point you can make your own. Let's look inside the box!

Hi, I'm Jeremy Ellis: High School Teacher and Builder

I like to look inside the box, and I want students to be able to do the same.



I'm a British Columbia technology teacher with about 35 years in the classroom, building open tools for WebAI, TinyML and robotics. My GitHub profile is hpssjellis. Combining TinyML and WebAI: webmcu-ai

One GitHub organization where the browser and the microcontroller work together.



Visit webmcu-ai.github.io and the repositories at github.com/webmcu-ai I combined TinyML and WebAI so that a web page can collect data, train a model and talk to the board, while the firmware stays a single readable file. Inspired by Vijay Reddi's tinyTorch and mlsysbook.ai, I adapted this philosophy into Arduino-compatible code with help from LLMs.

The ideas are described in two open preprints: 2604.23012 and 2604.22834.

Video 5: Sensor Fusion with the Nano 33 BLE Sense

Probably the most important video: many imperfect sensors, one webpage for ML training.

Video 5: Sensor Fusion with the Nano33BleSense and a webpage for ML training

▶


No single sensor tells the whole story. Fusion is how a small board gets a bigger picture. The Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense carries many sensors at once: motion, magnetic field, pressure, temperature, humidity, light, color, proximity, gesture and sound. Each one is noisy and limited. Together they can describe what is going on much more reliably.
